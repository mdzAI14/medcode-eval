- 46-year-old man, follow-up visit in the orthopedic clinic six weeks after an injury.
- Six weeks ago he fell from a ladder and had an open, displaced comminuted fracture of the shaft of the right tibia. The fibula was not broken.
- The same day the wound was cleaned and debrided and the fracture was fixed with an intramedullary nail.
- The wound over the shin was about 12 cm long with extensive damage to the skin and muscle. It could not be closed directly and was covered with a muscle flap five days after the injury.
- Today: mild aching in the leg, walking with crutches with partial weight on the leg, no fever.
- Examination: the wound and the flap are fully healed, no redness, no drainage; the leg is straight; sensation and pulses in the foot are normal.
- X-rays of the right tibia taken today and read by the surgeon: callus is forming, alignment is maintained, the nail is in good position.
- Assessment in the surgeon's words: open displaced comminuted fracture of the shaft of the right tibia, six weeks after intramedullary nailing, healing as expected.
- Plan: increase weight bearing as tolerated, physical therapy, return in six weeks with new X-rays.
- The note never names an open fracture type: no type number, no grade and no classification is given. Only the wound is described.
- No other injuries and no other medical problems. He takes acetaminophen as needed.
