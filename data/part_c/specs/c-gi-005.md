- 34-year-old woman, yearly follow-up visit in the gastroenterology clinic for celiac disease.
- Celiac disease diagnosed by biopsy two years ago. She keeps a strict gluten-free diet.
- Gastroenterologist's history: no diarrhea, no abdominal pain, no bloating. No rash.
- Results reviewed by the gastroenterologist: tissue transglutaminase antibody now in the normal range; blood count and iron studies normal.
- Examination by the gastroenterologist: abdomen soft, not tender.
- Assessment in the gastroenterologist's words: celiac disease, well controlled on a gluten-free diet.
- Plan by the gastroenterologist: continue the gluten-free diet, repeat antibody test in one year, return in one year.
- Dietitian's note, same visit, written by the registered dietitian: she has eaten about half of her usual intake for three months; nutrition diagnosis: moderate protein-calorie malnutrition; plan: an oral nutrition supplement twice a day and a diet review in six weeks.
- The gastroenterologist's part of the note does not mention malnutrition, weight or food intake. The malnutrition diagnosis appears only in the dietitian's note. The note gives no weight and no body mass index.
- No other medical problems and no regular medicines.
