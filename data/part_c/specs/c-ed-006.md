- 74-year-old woman, emergency department visit.
- Three days of increasing shortness of breath and swelling of both legs. She now sleeps on three pillows. No chest pain and no fever.
- She has chronic diastolic heart failure and has had hypertension for many years. She takes furosemide, lisinopril and amlodipine every day.
- No chronic kidney disease, no diabetes, no coronary artery disease and no atrial fibrillation. No other medical problems.
- Examination: blood pressure 152/88, heart rate 92, oxygen saturation 93 percent on room air; crackles at both lung bases; swelling of both legs to the knees.
- Results: chest X-ray read by the emergency physician shows pulmonary congestion; B-type natriuretic peptide elevated; troponin normal; kidney function normal; electrocardiogram shows sinus rhythm with no acute changes.
- Diagnosis in the emergency physician's words, as two separate items: (1) acute on chronic diastolic heart failure; (2) hypertension.
- Treatment and disposition: intravenous furosemide in the department, with good urine output and easier breathing; discharged home with a higher dose of furosemide; cardiology follow-up in three days.
- The physician does not say that the heart failure is due to the hypertension and does not use the words hypertensive heart disease. Nothing in the note says that the two are unrelated.
