- 57-year-old woman, visit in the orthopedic hand clinic.
- Two months of catching and locking of the ring finger, worse in the morning. At times she has to straighten the finger with her other hand. No injury.
- Surgeon's examination: a tender nodule in the palm at the base of the ring finger; the finger locks in flexion and releases with a click; the other fingers and the thumb move normally.
- Assessment in the surgeon's words: trigger finger of the ring finger.
- Treatment: the surgeon gave a corticosteroid injection into the tendon sheath at this visit.
- Nurse's procedure entry, written by the clinic nurse at the same visit: assisted with a corticosteroid injection into the tendon sheath of the right ring finger; site cleaned; bandage applied to the right palm; the patient tolerated the procedure well.
- Plan: return in six weeks; surgical release if the locking comes back.
- The surgeon's part of the note never names the hand: no right, no left and no dominant hand. The side appears only in the nurse's entry, and nothing in the note conflicts with it.
- No diabetes and no rheumatoid arthritis. No other medical problems and no regular medicines.
