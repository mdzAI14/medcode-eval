- 19-year-old woman, same-day visit in the ear, nose and throat clinic.
- Two days of sore throat, pain on swallowing and fever. First episode of tonsillitis.
- No cough, no rash, no trouble opening her mouth, no change in her voice, no trouble breathing.
- Vital signs recorded by the nurse: temperature 38.6 C, heart rate 96, blood pressure 118/72.
- Point-of-care test done and recorded by the nurse: rapid streptococcal antigen test positive.
- Examination by the physician: both tonsils red and enlarged with white exudate, uvula in the midline, soft palate does not bulge.
- Assessment in the physician's words: acute tonsillitis.
- Plan: amoxicillin for ten days, fluids, ibuprofen as needed, return if swallowing or breathing becomes difficult.
- The physician's part of the note does not mention the test result and names no organism. The result appears only in the nurse's entry.
- No other medical problems, no regular medicines, no drug allergies.
