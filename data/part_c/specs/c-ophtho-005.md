- 58-year-old man, follow-up visit in the eye clinic for retinopathy of both eyes, which was first found at an eye examination one year ago. The visit is a follow-up of that condition, not a routine examination.
- He has had hypertension for ten years and takes lisinopril every day. He has no diabetes.
- No change in vision, no headache.
- Blood pressure measured by the technician today: 168/98.
- Visual acuity 20/20 in each eye. Intraocular pressure 15 mm Hg in each eye.
- Dilated fundus examination by the ophthalmologist, both eyes: narrowed arterioles, arteriovenous nicking, scattered flame-shaped hemorrhages and several cotton wool spots. The optic discs are flat with sharp margins.
- Assessment in the ophthalmologist's words, as two separate items: (1) retinopathy, both eyes; (2) hypertension, blood pressure high today.
- Plan: he is asked to see his primary care physician this week about his blood pressure; return in three months for a repeat dilated examination.
- The ophthalmologist does not use the words hypertensive retinopathy and does not say that the retinopathy is due to, caused by or associated with the hypertension.
- No other eye disease and no other medical problems.
