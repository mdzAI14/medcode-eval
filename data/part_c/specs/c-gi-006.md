- 44-year-old man, visit in the gastroenterology clinic.
- Three months of bright red blood on the toilet paper after bowel movements. He feels a lump at the anus that comes out when he strains and goes back by itself. No pain.
- No change in bowel habit and no weight loss. A colonoscopy eight months ago was normal.
- Examination by the gastroenterologist: abdomen soft, not tender. Anoscopy: internal hemorrhoids; with straining they prolapse beyond the anal verge and then retract without help; no thrombosis, no fissure, no external hemorrhoids and no skin tags.
- Blood count normal.
- Assessment in the gastroenterologist's words: bleeding internal hemorrhoids that prolapse with straining but retract spontaneously.
- Plan: fiber supplement and more fluids; rubber band ligation if the bleeding continues; return in two months.
- The note never names a degree, a grade or a stage of the hemorrhoids. The assessment itself describes how they behave.
- No other medical problems and no regular medicines.
