- 34-year-old man, emergency department visit.
- Five days of cough with yellow sputum. No fever and no shortness of breath at rest.
- He had asthma as a child. No symptoms and no inhalers since age 12.
- He has never smoked.
- Examination: temperature 37.2 C, heart rate 88, respiratory rate 18, oxygen saturation 97 percent on room air, speaks in full sentences, scattered expiratory wheezes in both lungs, no crackles.
- Chest X-ray read by the emergency physician: no infiltrate.
- Treatment: one albuterol nebulizer treatment; the wheezes cleared and he felt better.
- Diagnosis in the emergency physician's words: acute bronchitis.
- The physician does not diagnose asthma or an asthma exacerbation. Asthma appears only in the history, as a condition he had as a child.
- Discharged with an albuterol inhaler to use as needed for one week.
- No other medical problems and no regular medicines.
