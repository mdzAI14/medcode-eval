- 68-year-old man, same-day visit in the eye clinic.
- He woke this morning unable to see with the right eye apart from light and movement. No pain.
- No headache, no scalp tenderness, no jaw pain when chewing. No earlier episodes.
- Examination of the right eye: front of the eye normal; dilated fundus examination shows diffuse whitening of the retina with a cherry-red spot at the macula and narrowed arterioles.
- Examination of the left eye: normal.
- Assessment in the ophthalmologist's words: sudden visual loss, right eye; findings compatible with central retinal artery occlusion.
- Plan: sent directly to the emergency department for stroke evaluation; the emergency physician was called.
- The ophthalmologist gives central retinal artery occlusion only with the words compatible with, never as an established diagnosis.
- The note records no measured visual acuity and does not use the words blind, blindness or low vision.
- No known medical problems and no regular medicines.
