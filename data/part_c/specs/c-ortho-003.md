- 58-year-old man, new patient visit in the orthopedic clinic.
- One week of pain and swelling at the back of the left ankle, worse with walking and on stairs.
- No injury, no fall and no change in his activity.
- He finished a ten-day course of levofloxacin six days ago, prescribed by his primary care physician. No other medicines.
- Examination by the surgeon: left Achilles tendon thickened and tender about 4 cm above the heel, no gap in the tendon, calf squeeze test normal, plantar flexion strength full, right Achilles tendon normal.
- Ultrasound of the left Achilles tendon done in clinic and read by the surgeon: thickening of the tendon without a tear.
- Assessment in the surgeon's words: Achilles tendinitis, left.
- Plan: heel lift, relative rest, physical therapy, return in four weeks.
- The surgeon states no cause for the tendinitis and does not connect it to the levofloxacin. The note does not name the infection the levofloxacin was given for.
- No chronic medical problems.
