- 63-year-old man, new patient visit in the ear, nose and throat clinic.
- Trouble hearing in both ears that has come on slowly over several years.
- He worked for thirty years in a metal stamping plant with loud machinery and wore hearing protection only some of the time. He retired two years ago.
- No ear pain, no discharge, no ringing in the ears, no dizziness, no ear surgery.
- Examination: ear canals and eardrums normal in both ears; tuning fork tests show the Weber test in the midline and air conduction better than bone conduction in both ears.
- Audiogram today, read by the physician: sensorineural hearing loss in both ears, symmetric, with a notch at 4000 hertz reaching 55 decibels; word recognition 88 percent in each ear.
- Assessment in the physician's words: sensorineural hearing loss, bilateral.
- Plan: referred for a hearing aid evaluation, repeat audiogram in one year.
- The physician states no cause for the hearing loss and does not connect it to noise or to his work.
- No other medical problems and no regular medicines.
