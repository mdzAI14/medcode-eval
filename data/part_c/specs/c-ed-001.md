- 41-year-old man, emergency department visit.
- Two days of vomiting and watery diarrhea. Two people in his household had the same illness this week.
- He is drinking fluids and keeping most of them down. No blood in the stool.
- Examination: temperature 37.4 C, heart rate 88, blood pressure 122/76, mucous membranes moist, abdomen soft with mild diffuse tenderness and no guarding.
- Laboratory results: sodium 138 mmol/L, potassium 4.0 mmol/L, bicarbonate 22 mmol/L, creatinine 1.6 mg/dL. The last creatinine on file, three months ago, was 0.9 mg/dL.
- Treatment: ondansetron by mouth. He drank fluids without vomiting. The physician records no signs of dehydration. No intravenous fluids.
- Diagnosis in the emergency physician's words: viral gastroenteritis. It is the only diagnosis.
- The creatinine values appear only in the laboratory results. The physician states no kidney diagnosis and does not comment on the creatinine.
- Discharged home with oral fluids and return precautions.
- No medical problems and no regular medicines.
