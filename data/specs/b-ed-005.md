- 49-year-old female presents to the emergency department with 10 hours of right upper abdominal pain that began after a meal, with nausea, two episodes of vomiting and a low-grade fever.
- This is her first emergency department visit for this problem.
- Vital signs: temperature 38.0 C, heart rate 96, blood pressure 128/78.
- Examination: right upper quadrant tenderness; positive Murphy sign.
- WBC 13.8 x 10^9/L. Bilirubin normal. Lipase normal.
- Ultrasound: gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening and fluid around the gallbladder; common bile duct of normal caliber with no stone; no obstruction.
- The provider lists two diagnoses: "Acute cholecystitis." and "Cholelithiasis."
- No other conditions are documented.
- Plan: general surgery consulted; admitted for cholecystectomy.
