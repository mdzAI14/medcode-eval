- 44-year-old male presents to the emergency department with 6 hours of severe left flank pain radiating to the groin, with nausea and one episode of vomiting.
- This is his first emergency department visit for this problem. No previous kidney stones.
- Vital signs: temperature 37.0 C, heart rate 98, blood pressure 138/84.
- Urinalysis: microscopic hematuria, no pyuria, negative nitrite.
- CT of the abdomen and pelvis without contrast: 5 mm stone in the left distal ureter with mild left hydronephrosis; no stone in either kidney; right side normal.
- The provider states the hydronephrosis is due to obstruction by the ureteral stone.
- The provider states there is no infection.
- The provider lists two diagnoses: "Left ureteral stone." and "Left hydronephrosis."
- No other conditions are documented.
- Plan: pain control, tamsulosin, urology follow-up; discharged home.
